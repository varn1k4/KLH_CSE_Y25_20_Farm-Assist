Modern wheat farming depends heavily on improved seed varieties and efficient irrigation scheduling.
Farmers monitor soil moisture and temperature closely to decide the best time for sowing wheat seeds.
Fertilizers containing nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium are applied at different growth stages of the wheat crop.
Fungicides are commonly used to control wheat rust and other fungal diseases that spread through leaves and stems.
Harvesting is usually done once the wheat grain turns golden and moisture content drops to a suitable level.
Proper drainage and organic compost application can significantly improve long-term soil fertility for wheat cultivation.
Climate change and irregular rainfall patterns pose ongoing challenges for wheat farmers around the world.
