Organic farming relies on natural methods such as compost, crop rotation, and biological pest control instead of synthetic chemicals.
Farmers practicing organic farming avoid chemical fertilizer and pesticide, focusing instead on soil fertility through organic matter.
Compost and manure are commonly used as natural fertilizer alternatives in organic farming systems.
Organic farming practices often result in improved long-term soil health and reduced environmental impact.
Weed and pest management in organic farming rely on mulching, natural predators, and crop rotation techniques.
Consumers increasingly prefer organic produce due to concerns about chemical residues and environmental sustainability.
Transitioning to organic farming requires patience, as soil fertility and productivity improve gradually over several seasons.
