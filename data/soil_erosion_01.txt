Soil erosion occurs when topsoil is removed by wind, water, or poor farming practices, reducing farmland fertility.
Farmers can prevent soil erosion through practices such as contour farming, mulching, and cover cropping.
Heavy rainfall on bare or poorly managed farmland significantly increases the risk of soil erosion.
Erosion removes valuable nutrients and organic matter, making soil less productive for future crop growth.
Planting trees and maintaining vegetation cover around farmland can help reduce wind and water erosion.
Terracing on sloped farmland is a traditional technique used to control soil erosion and conserve water.
Preventing soil erosion is essential for maintaining long-term agricultural productivity and soil fertility.
