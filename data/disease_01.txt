Plant diseases caused by fungi, bacteria, and viruses can severely affect crop growth and yield.
Common agricultural diseases include blight, rust, and bacterial leaf spot that damage leaves, stems, and roots.
Farmers use fungicides to control fungal diseases and prevent them from spreading across farmland.
Humid climate conditions and poor drainage often increase the risk of plant disease outbreaks.
Early detection of disease symptoms such as leaf discoloration helps farmers take timely action.
Crop rotation and proper field sanitation reduce the chances of disease buildup in soil over multiple seasons.
Disease-resistant seed varieties are increasingly used to minimize crop loss and improve agricultural productivity.
