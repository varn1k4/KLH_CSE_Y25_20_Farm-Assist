Disease management in agriculture requires a combination of prevention, monitoring, and timely treatment.
Farmers apply fungicides and other treatments as soon as disease symptoms appear on plant leaves or stems.
Proper spacing between plants improves air circulation and reduces the spread of fungal diseases in crops.
Soil-borne diseases can persist for years, making crop rotation an important tool for long-term disease control.
Weather conditions such as excess rainfall and high humidity create favorable environments for disease-causing organisms.
Farmers who maintain good soil fertility and drainage often see fewer disease problems in their crops.
Integrated disease management combined with healthy farming practices helps protect crop yield and quality.
