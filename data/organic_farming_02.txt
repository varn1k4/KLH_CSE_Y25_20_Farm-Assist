Organic certification requires farmers to follow strict guidelines regarding fertilizer, pesticide, and seed usage.
Organic farming emphasizes biodiversity, healthy soil ecosystems, and sustainable water and irrigation management.
Farmers use cover crops and mulching to naturally suppress weeds and improve soil fertility in organic systems.
Disease and pest control in organic farming often relies on natural predators and resistant seed varieties.
Organic compost improves soil structure, water retention, and nutrient availability for healthy crop growth.
Although organic farming yields may initially be lower, long-term soil fertility often improves significantly.
Sustainable organic practices support both agricultural productivity and environmental conservation for future generations.
